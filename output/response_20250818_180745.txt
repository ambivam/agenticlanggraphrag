Query: tell me about hindi cinemas

Response:
From knowledge base: Hindi cinema, known globally as Bollywood, is a major segment of the Indian film industry, centered in Mumbai. The term "Bollywood" is a blend of Bombay (Mumbai's former name) and Hollywood, the center of the American film industry. Hindi cinema primarily produces films in Hindi and has been a significant part of Indian culture and entertainment.

### Historical Overview

- **Golden Age (Late 1940s to Early 1960s):** This era is celebrated for producing some of the most iconic films in Bollywood history, marking the industry's coming of age post-India's independence. Films like "Pyaasa" (1957) and "Shree 420" (1955) are notable examples of this period, showcasing the blend of social issues with cinematic artistry.
  
- **1970s Paradigm Shift:** The industry faced thematic stagnation until the emergence of the screenwriting duo Salim–Javed, who introduced the genre of gritty, violent films based on the Bombay underworld. This era began with films like "Zanjeer" (1973) and "Deewaar" (1975), setting new trends in Hindi cinema.

### Industry Scale and Impact

- **Production Volume:** In 2017, the Indian cinema industry produced 1,986 feature films, with Hindi cinema contributing the largest share of 364 films.
  
- **Box Office Revenue:** In 2019, Hindi films sold approximately 341 million tickets in India. By 2022, Hindi cinema accounted for 33% of the box office revenue in India, the largest share compared to other regional cinemas.

- **Global Influence:** Mumbai, the heart of Hindi cinema, is one of the largest centers for film production globally. The industry has a significant impact not just in India but also internationally, contributing to its worldwide fanbase and influence.

### Cultural Significance

Hindi cinema has played a pivotal role in shaping Indian culture, politics, and society. It has been a medium for storytelling that combines entertainment with social commentary, often reflecting the societal changes and challenges of its times. The widespread appeal of Bollywood films can be attributed to their unique blend of music, dance, drama, and spectacle, making them a significant cultural export of India.

In summary, Hindi cinema or Bollywood is not just an entertainment industry; it is a cultural phenomenon that has evolved over the years, mirroring and sometimes shaping societal attitudes and norms. Its global appeal and influence continue to grow, making it an essential part of the global cinematic landscape.
